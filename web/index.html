Inference

An isotope measurement defines a family of compatible pCO2, GPP, and pO2 states. A one-coordinate solution is reported only after the other two coordinates are constrained.

Frequently asked questions

Why must two variables be constrained before solving for the third?

Atmospheric O2 Δ′17O does not uniquely determine pCO2, GPP, and pO2 simultaneously. Independent constraints on two coordinates restrict the family of compatible atmospheric states and allow the remaining coordinate to be inferred.

What does the solution uncertainty interval include?

The displayed interval combines analytical isotope uncertainty with the two coordinate constraints entered by the user. A value ± 1σ is treated as Gaussian and an exact range as uniform within its bounds. Structural model uncertainty is assessed separately.

How is the probability distribution calculated?

The model is evaluated across its accepted domain and weighted by the isotope analytical likelihood and entered constraints. Uncertainty in the other coordinates is integrated out. The solved coordinate has a uniform prior in its reported units; a stated range is uniform within its bounds and a 1σ constraint is Gaussian. The displayed profile focuses on the central 99.8% of posterior probability.

What is the difference between the isotope field and the constrained solution?

The isotope field shows deterministic central-model Δ′17O predictions across pCO2 and GPP at one fixed pO2. The constrained solver combines an isotope measurement and its analytical uncertainty with independent constraints to produce a probability distribution for one requested coordinate.

What assumptions are made about input uncertainties?

The isotope measurements and entered coordinate constraints are treated as statistically independent. Correlations or covariance between inputs are not included by the interface and require a separate joint analysis.

What does a boundary-limited solution mean?

Appreciable posterior probability reaches an edge of the accepted model domain. The reported interval is therefore limited by that boundary and should not be interpreted as evidence that the true value lies at the boundary.

Does the main solver represent steady state?

Yes. The isotope field and constrained solver use steady-state model outputs. Time-dependent adjustment after a change in pCO2, GPP, pO2, or photosynthesis is calculated separately in the Time response view.

How should the time-response experiments be interpreted?

Each experiment applies an imposed change at year zero; negative displayed times show the initial state. Operational equilibrium is reached when both isotope coordinates enter and remain within 0.001‰ of the long-run state. The equilibrium search is independent of the displayed duration and extends to 100,000 years.

Can the model be applied to ancient atmospheres?

Yes, when the requested state lies inside the accepted model domain and independent evidence constrains the other atmospheric coordinates. Geological interpretations should also consider structural model uncertainty and the applicability of the model assumptions to the investigated interval.

How are cosmic spherules converted?

I-type cosmic spherule measurements are converted to atmospheric O2 using Zahnow et al. (2025), Eq. 3, calibrated by Fischer et al. (2021).

How should cosmic-spherule uncertainties be entered?

Enter the analytical 1σ uncertainties of both spherule Δ′17O and δ18O. They are propagated through the atmospheric conversion. Calibration-parameter sensitivity is assessed separately and is not added to the displayed analytical 1σ interval.

What happens outside the model domain?

The calculation is rejected. The accepted surface is not numerically extrapolated.
