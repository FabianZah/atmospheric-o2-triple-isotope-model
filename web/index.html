Frequently asked questions

Why must two variables be constrained before solving for the third?

One atmospheric O2 Δ′17O observation defines a family of compatible pCO2, GPP, and pO2 states. Independent constraints on two coordinates restrict that family and allow the remaining coordinate to be inferred.

What does the solution uncertainty interval include?

The displayed interval combines analytical isotope uncertainty with the two coordinate constraints and any sulfate-process constraints entered by the user. A fixed value is held constant, a value ± 1σ is treated as Gaussian, and an exact range as uniform within its bounds. These inputs are treated as independent, with independent Gaussian errors for the isotope measurements. Structural model uncertainty is assessed separately.

How is the probability distribution calculated?

Candidate atmospheres are weighted by agreement with the isotope measurement and entered constraints. The calculation averages over the allowed values of the other coordinates to obtain the distribution for the requested variable. That variable has a uniform prior in its reported units across the accepted domain. For pCO2, the plotted curve shows probability per equal log10(pCO2) interval to match the logarithmic display axis; the inference prior remains uniform in ppm.

What is the difference between the isotope field and the constrained solution?

The isotope field shows deterministic central-model Δ′17O predictions across pCO2 and GPP at one fixed pO2. The constrained solver combines an isotope measurement and its analytical uncertainty with independent constraints to produce a probability distribution for one requested coordinate.

What do model limits and boundary-limited results mean?

The solver shows the accepted input ranges and flags entries outside them. A boundary-limited result occurs when appreciable posterior probability reaches an edge of the accepted model domain. Compatible states may extend beyond that edge, so the inferred interval is only partially constrained. A peak at the boundary should be interpreted together with the warning and the entered constraints.

How do steady-state results and time-response experiments differ?

The isotope field and model solver describe steady states. Select O₂ isotope composition to predict atmospheric Δ′17O and δ18O from pCO2, GPP, and pO2. Uncertain input constraints produce isotope medians and central 95% propagated intervals. Time response follows adjustment after an imposed change in pCO2, GPP, pO2, or photosynthesis. Year zero marks the start of the change, including a gradual pCO2 trajectory; negative times show the initial state. Operational equilibrium is reached when both isotope coordinates enter and remain within 0.001‰ of the long-run state. The equilibrium search is independent of the displayed duration and extends to 100,000 years.

How long do calculations take?

Most fixed-constraint calculations finish within seconds. Narrow probability fields may require automatic grid refinement and take a few minutes. Time-response experiments solve adaptive differential equations; the photosynthesis step integrates the coupled carbon, O2, and isotope response over the equilibrium-search horizon. Runtime also depends on the selected constraints, experiment duration, and server load. When the server is busy, a waiting indicator appears and the calculation starts automatically when capacity becomes available.

Can the model be applied to ancient atmospheres?

Yes, when the requested state lies inside the accepted model domain and independent evidence constrains the other atmospheric coordinates. Geological interpretations should also consider structural model uncertainty and the applicability of the model assumptions to the investigated interval.

How are cosmic-spherule inputs converted?

Enter the I-type cosmic spherule's Δ′17O and δ18O with their analytical 1σ uncertainties. The measurements and uncertainties are converted to atmospheric O2 using Zahnow et al. (2025), Eq. 3, calibrated by Fischer et al. (2021). Calibration-parameter sensitivity is assessed separately.

What does the sulfate input require?

Enter the sulfate isotope measurements and uncertainties, the air-derived oxygen percentage, and the non-air component's Δ′17O after sulfate formation and exchange. Both process inputs accept a fixed value, 1σ uncertainty or exact range. Choose constraints appropriate to a preserved or independently reconstructed primary sulfate signal. The prefilled isotope values and analytical errors are illustrative examples.

Why is there no sulfate non-air δ18O input?

For each candidate atmosphere, the solver derives it by oxygen-isotope mass balance from measured sulfate δ18O, the air-derived oxygen fraction, atmospheric δ18O and the selected incorporation treatment. The non-air component's Δ′17O is supplied separately. Its inferred δ18O should be plausible for the sample's formation environment.

Which sulfate O2 incorporation treatment should I use?

The default transfers atmospheric O2's isotope composition unchanged. The irreversible and equilibrated options apply the 25 °C isotope effects calculated by Peng et al. (2026) for sulfite oxidation, with a fixed 25% contribution of O2-derived oxygen. Choose the treatment that matches the sample's formation process, or use custom shifts supported by independent evidence. The non-air oxygen composition is constrained separately.

What do the sulfate custom isotope shifts mean?

Each shift is the isotope composition of the transferred oxygen minus that of atmospheric O2, before mixing with non-air oxygen. The δ′18O shift equals 1000 ln α18; the Δ′17O shift uses slope 0.528. Enter shifts supported by the sample's formation conditions.
